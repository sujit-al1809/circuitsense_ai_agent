## Overview

A resistor converts current into a proportional voltage (Ohm's law, V = I × R). In circuits it is used
to limit current, divide voltages, bias active devices and set amplifier gain.

## Ratings (typical, illustrative)

- Power rating: 0.25 W at 70 °C ambient, derated linearly to 0 W at about 155 °C.
- Tolerance: ±5 % (carbon film) or ±1 % (metal film).
- Good practice: keep dissipation below about half of the rating for reliability.

## Colour code and markings

Four-band parts: two digits, multiplier, tolerance. 330 Ω = orange-orange-brown-gold;
10 kΩ = brown-black-orange-gold; 4.7 kΩ = yellow-violet-red-gold; 1 kΩ = brown-black-red-gold.
Schematics often use RKM notation: 4k7 = 4.7 kΩ, 2R2 = 2.2 Ω.

## Measuring resistance

Always remove power before measuring resistance. In-circuit readings can be lower than the marked value
because other parts form parallel paths; lift one lead for a definitive reading. A reading of OL (open)
means the resistor or a joint is open.

## Failure modes

Overloaded resistors discolour, crack or go open-circuit. Film resistors rarely fail short.
A value that has drifted high is a common cause of low current or low divider output.
